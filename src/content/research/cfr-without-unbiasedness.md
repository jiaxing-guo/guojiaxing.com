## Central Question

When can persistent, conditionally biased public-chance feedback still give valid Counterfactual Regret Minimization guarantees?

## Main Contribution

The paper establishes a deterministic target-transfer theorem for uniform, nonnested additive public cuts. The result combines delivered-feedback regret with a public-debit term that tracks coverage discrepancy and strategy motion.

Consecutively balanced schedules converge for additive signed regret matching and RM+ under predetermined averaging weights. A component-resolved form converts an execution trace into a numerical exploitability certificate.

On two heads-up no-limit hold'em turn endgames, persistent order improves over fresh reshuffling. The depth study identifies a crossover between 32 and 64 full-cut outcome budgets.
